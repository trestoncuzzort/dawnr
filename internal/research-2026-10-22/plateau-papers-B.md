# Plateau papers B: why supervised fine-tuning plateaus on a verified-program task, and what breaks the plateau

- Date (UTC): 2026-10-22 10:39 UTC
- Question: why SFT of a small model plateaus when it must write a Python solution, a formal specification in the small new language t, and a proof checked by seven verifiers (dawnr v5: Qwen3.5-4B student, QLoRA rank 64, 4,189 SFT rows), and which published results have broken comparable plateaus.
- Method (query counts): arXiv export API, 185 queries (Q01 to Q130 in eight tag blocks C1 to C8, then 55 title lookups B01 to B55), up to 50 results each. Pacing was 3 to 4 seconds at first and 8 seconds after the API began returning HTTP 429 rate limits; four queries that hit 429 were retried after waits, and all 185 completed. The queries returned 4237 result rows and 3661 distinct arXiv IDs; 3661 distinct candidates were screened by title and abstract, and the seen IDs are kept in /tmp/pb/seen.txt. WebSearch, 4 queries: for Reflexion and CRITIC, which the API did not return; for the Thinking Machines LoRA post; and for a search snippet on the self-repair paper (2306.09896), whose 34% figure is marked (snippet). WebFetch, 2 pages (the Thinking Machines post and the arXiv abstract of Reflexion). Years are the arXiv first-version year. Venues come from the arXiv journal_ref field, else from an "accepted at" phrase in the comment field, else "arXiv"; the CRITIC venue comes from a search result. Figures in the why column are quoted from the abstract, or from the fetched page where marked (fetched); a figure that came only from a search snippet is marked (snippet). Venue names were not checked one by one against proceedings.
- Verification: 250 of 250 links returned HTTP 200 when checked at 10:37 UTC (249 of 249 arXiv abstract pages and 1 of 1 blog page).
- Tag key: C1 SFT saturation and scaling; C2 self-generated data collapse and verifier filtering (includes test-fitting, reward hacking on tests, and RL or repeated sampling rewarded by a verifier); C3 unfamiliar language and representation (grammar-constrained decoding, syntax-aware training, documentation and retrieval, transfer from Dafny, Lean or F*); C4 self-repair and self-refine limits by model size; C5 teacher distillation and rejection sampling (includes iterative verifier-filtered self-training); C6 adapter capacity (LoRA versus full fine-tuning, rank, forgetting); C7 data quality, curriculum and selection; C8 specification synthesis (postconditions, loop invariants, verified code, and the proof models that feed them).
