# Plateau papers A: what moved small models on formal specification and proof writing

- Date (UTC): 2026-10-22 10:34 UTC
- Question: what has measurably moved small models (up to about 8B) on formal specification writing and proof generation, and by which mechanism. The question comes from dawnr v5, a Qwen3.5-4B student (QLoRA rank 64, 4,189 SFT rows) that writes a Python solution, then a specification in the t language, then a proof that seven kernels check. On the 95-problem development panel it ends fully clean on about 6 problems. The three data rows tried this week moved nothing, and the ceiling instrument finds a reference-correct specification within 20 samples for only 29 of the 63 problems the student solves in Python. Sampling noise alone is about plus or minus 2 problems.
- Method (query counts): arXiv export API, 130 queries of which 130 returned results (some only after repeated HTTP 429 responses), up to 50 results each by relevance, 1183 unique arXiv IDs (the first pass ran 3 s apart; after the first 429s the rest ran 6 s and then 20 s apart). WebSearch, 9 standard-mode calls, for venues and for named works the API had not returned; WebFetch, 4 calls that read arXiv abstract pages for four named works. Semantic Scholar, 2 calls, both HTTP 429, not used. Nothing was cloned. Deduplication: arXiv ID with version stripped, then normalized title for web items. Years are the arXiv first-version year. Venues come from the arXiv journal_ref or comment field, or from a web result; "arXiv" means the arXiv record names no venue, not that none exists. Every number in a why cell was checked by script against the stored text: the arXiv abstract, or for one named work with no arXiv abstract in the results (a search paraphrase, marked in its cell); search snippets are marked wherever they are used.
- Tag key: M1 more data (more training examples or synthetic problems); M2 data quality and verifier filtering of training examples; M3 RL or expert iteration with a verifier reward; M4 test-time search: best-of-n, tree search, repair loops, or a verifier used inside generation; M5 base model size and capacity; M6 representation of an unfamiliar formal language; M7 evaluation and measurement.
- Totals: Table 1 has 250 papers (250 with an arXiv link, 0 from other web sources). Tags, counted over the table (a row with two tags counts once under each): M1 41, M2 28, M3 57, M4 122, M5 52, M6 24, M7 89.
