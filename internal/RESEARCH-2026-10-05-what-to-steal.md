# What to take from other people's open work (sweep of 2026-10-05)

The operator, 2026-10-05: "can we do an ultracode to find as many projects to steal from that will boost our goals
and maybe change our perspective on our work", after "if we can take the ambition and turn it into reality with the
groundbreaking work of other developers and engineers we can make something that works for everyone". This file is
that sweep: what was found, what it says dawnr has been seeing wrongly or narrowly, and the order in which things
are taken. The full result, field by field, is `internal/data/what-to-steal-2026-10-05.json`.

## How it was made, and how far to trust it

Eight scouts, one for each part of the ambition, each told what dawnr is, where it is weak, what it already uses,
and the rule for taking: code, weights or data dawnr would ship or train on must be Apache-2.0, MIT, BSD, ISC, CC0
or CC-BY; anything else is an idea to learn from; data written by GPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, Llama or Gemma models
is never training data; nothing from memory, only from pages fetched in the session. A ninth agent then fetched
the page and the licence of every find marked "take first" and tried to refute it. Nine agents, 452 page fetches
and searches, about 800,000 tokens, ten minutes.

- **127 distinct finds** (46 code, 27 methods, 19 weights, 18 datasets, 17 ideas only); ten were found by two
  scouts independently.
- **23 marked "take first" were re-checked.** 18 stand as takeable, 5 are ideas only (no licence on the code, or a
  paper, or a proprietary service), none was dropped. In 9 of the 23 the checker could confirm only part of the
  number the scout quoted; those are marked "claim partly" in section 3.
- **The other 104 carry the licence their scout read and were not re-checked.** Before any of them is used, its
  page and licence are fetched again and filed on a research receipt, as for every fix (`AGENTS.md`).
- Every number in this file is the source's own claim about itself, on its own data. None is a dawnr measurement.

## 1. What should change how the work is seen

Each of these is a claim with the evidence a scout brought back, what dawnr already has that bears on it, and what
changes. They are ordered by how much they would move, not by area.

**1. The answer rate is a search budget before it is a training problem.** With a checker that picks the winner,
the share of problems solved rises with the number of samples drawn, about linearly in the logarithm (15.9% to 56%
from 1 to 250 samples on SWE-bench Lite, arXiv:2407.21787), where voting and learned rankers flatten out.
Reinforcement learning against a verifier mostly raises the first sample and can lower what many samples reach
(a base model at 50% pass@128 against 42.8% for its RL version, arXiv:2504.13837); distillation raises every k.
dawnr has the checker and has always reported at fixed budgets (17 answers on the scoreboard, 5 in `dawnr ask`).
*What changes:* the curve of problems proved against answers drawn is computed from the graded sets already on
disk before another training round is planned, and `dawnr ask` gets a budget the person can raise.

**2. A specification should be agreed with the person through examples, and a refusal should become a question.**
Showing a person a few concrete input and output pairs to approve, chosen where candidate programs disagree,
added about 46 points of first-answer accuracy within five interactions (TiCoder, arXiv:2404.10100; simulated
users, large models), and people judged the code better with less effort. `dawnr ask` today needs `--test`
lines, which a person who does not program cannot write, and refuses a question that "can be read more than one
way". *What changes:* the examples are proposed by dawnr and approved or corrected by the person; an ambiguous
question is answered with "should f([3, 1]) be [1, 3]?". The tool-call check built the same day already works
this way (it asks for the parameter by name).

**3. For everyday questions the right move may be to ship the rules and let the model fill in only the facts.**
With statutes already written as logic by hand, a model that only had to read the case reached 86 of 100 tax
cases, at a break-even cost of $15.78 a return against $270 for a human preparer (arXiv:2508.21051); on
TaxCalcBench the best frontier model, with web search, is right on 66% of returns. Catala (Apache-2.0) compiles law written
beside its legal text to Python and, since 1.3.0, emits a JSON trace of every rule applied. dawnr's own record
says writing a right and complete specification is the bottleneck; here a person has already written it.
*What changes:* a new kind of answer, where `extract` reads the facts with their sentences, a rule library
computes, and the trace is the certificate. It is also an audience nobody here had named: legal aid, benefits
offices, anyone who has to apply written rules.

**4. A "proved" count can rise for the wrong reason, and the field has measured how.** Rewarded only for
verifying, a model learned to drop specification clauses until a prover-checked reward required its specification
to imply the reference's (Re:Form, arXiv:2507.16331; two scouts read this in the paper, and the checker confirmed
the repository's licence but did not find the passage in what it fetched); given computable specifications, a
model copied the specification into the body and proved it by rewriting (CLEVER, arXiv:2505.13938, as one scout
read it). dawnr found two of its own
versions of this by hand (the row audit, and specifications that enumerate the examples, `CORRECTIONS.md`) and
holds specifications to mutated outputs and larger inputs. *What changes:* two measurements are added to the
scoreboard's instrument, how often a proved program merely restates its specification and how many mutated
programs a specification rejects, and any reinforcement round uses a prover-checked strength term, with a
random-reward control and a second model family (a Qwen base gained 21 points from random rewards,
arXiv:2506.10947).

**5. Answer-or-refuse is one choice among several exact ones.** A shipped checker reports valid, invalid, true
only under named assumptions, and ambiguous (AWS Automated Reasoning checks; proprietary, idea only); conformal
back-off keeps the part of an answer that clears a guarantee (arXiv:2402.10978). *What changes:* where the full
specification is not proved but a part is, dawnr can say exactly that ("proved: the result is sorted; not
proved: it has the same elements"), labelled, instead of refusing. Nothing unproved is passed off; more that is
proved is shown.

**6. The lasting thing may be the checker, in front of any model.** A 59 million pound research programme stopped
funding specialised models and kept funding the assurance tooling, because general models moved faster than it
expected; browsers and operating systems are starting to ship a local model themselves; the open projects that
won users did it as a backend other programs select. dawnr already has the pieces (a writer elsewhere with the
gate here, MCP tools, an OpenAI-style endpoint, certificates that replay without the model). *What changes:* the
checker and the certificate are documented and tested as usable on their own, dawnr is tried as a provider
inside one or two programs people already run, and the published model is described as one writer among several.

**7. For extraction a small span reader may do better than the 4B writing words.** A 0.2B extractive reader
reports 83.8 exact match on SQuAD 2.0's development set (deepset/deberta-v3-base-squad2, CC-BY-4.0), and it can
only return a span of the source. dawnr's three measurements the same day put the 4B at 41 to 43% when held to a
span and 63 to 66% otherwise. The reader was trained on SQuAD, so that panel flatters it. *What changes:* it is
measured as an arm on a panel it was not trained on before the extraction path is rebuilt again.

**8. A transcript is a model's text, and the check for speech is alignment.** About 1% of Whisper transcripts
contained invented phrases (arXiv:2402.08021). Aligning a given text to the audio is far easier than writing it:
98% precision with the true text against 86% when the text came from a recogniser (NeMo Forced Aligner,
Interspeech 2023). That is the same asymmetry dawnr relies on for programs. *What changes:* speech enters the
installed command as "a quote from a recording carries its time and its alignment score, or is refused", with
silence removed before recognition, not as a second recogniser agreeing with the first. For scans the same
argument gives two independent readers that must agree, or the page shown beside the quote.

**9. Repair pays when the checker localises the failure, and only sound feedback helps.** Asking a small model to
fix a whole program from an error message is worth little at matched cost (arXiv:2306.09896); cutting the failing
step out as a hole and resampling only that took one prover from 25,600 samples to about 362 (APOLLO, MIT);
self-critique lowered accuracy where a sound external checker raised it (arXiv:2402.08115). Deterministic repairs
are cheaper still: dropping loop-invariant clauses that fail (Houdini), and bounded mutation of a specification's
operators, added 61 of 279 verified programs in SpecGen. The diagnostic of 2026-10-05 agrees from our side:
holding the output to the grammar fixed parsing and solved nothing more. *What changes:* the repair stage owed in
the plan is built from prover output only, localised, with the deterministic repairs first. *Measured the same
day* (`t/PREDICT-2026-10-05-invariant-repair.md`): dropping and reordering invariants by rule repaired 12 of 755
unproved loop answers, and the proofs it recovered were mostly of specifications that do not stand; it is not in
the gate. In 484 of the 494 answers where Dafny pointed at an invariant, the proof failed somewhere else once it
was dropped. The deterministic repair is not where the proofs are; the localised resampling is what is left to try.

**10. "Seven provers" is fewer than seven independent judgements.** Several rest on the same SMT solver, and
soundness bugs have been found in verifiers and compilers (arXiv:2512.05262); the same problems are proved about
five times as often in Dafny as in Lean (AlgoVeri). *What changes:* the certificate says which trusted base each
verdict rests on, a second kernel re-checks where one exists (nanoda for Lean, the Rocq checker, Carcara for
SMT proofs), and rates are reported by prover.

**11. Whether an answer will be right is partly knowable before it is written.** A linear probe on a 4B model's
state before generation predicts first-attempt correctness at AUC 0.88 (arXiv:2606.14530). dawnr measured the same
thing from outside on 2026-10-01 (the model separates easy problems from hard ones at 0.86 and cannot tell its
right answer from its wrong one). *What changes:* the probe is not a check and never shows anything; it decides
where the minutes go, and lets a hopeless question be refused in seconds instead of after three minutes.

**12. The light checks can go where the provers cannot.** Quoting, extracting, exact arithmetic and the tool-call
check are plain code; a model runs in a browser tab or on a phone today (wllama, MIT; PocketPal, MIT), and the
provers do not travel. *What changes:* "dawnr in a tab" for the checks that need no prover is a real option for
people who will never run an installer, with the full gate on a machine that can hold it. One box serving a room
of phones over local Wi-Fi is the same idea, and closer to the spaceship than one laptop each.

**Smaller, and kept.** A licence tag on weights does not clear them: the training-data section has to be read
too (weights under Apache-2.0 trained on non-commercial data or on a closed model's output). A fine-tune on
domain documents can raise invented numbers sharply (5% to 82% in one finance study), so every document or
number fine-tune is checked for numbers not in the source before and after. A refusal has a price, and a gate
that refuses often can be scored with the cost of the human alternative beside the cost of an error. Contract
and finance reviewers already work by "quote the clause or say it is not there", and expert, permissively
licensed panels exist for them (CUAD, ContractNLI, TAT-QA, FinQA).

**What the sweep says dawnr already had right.** Verified end-to-end code generation is hard for every model
(the best frontier model proved 4.9% on VERINA; one problem of 161 on CLEVER), so a high refusal rate is the
state of the field and not a defect of a 4B model. Small models fine-tuned on verified in-domain data match far
larger ones on formal tasks. Accuracy against a gold answer overstates what can be trusted: up to half of
exactly right answers in one study could not be attributed to the passage, which is the case for quote-or-refuse.
One scout read `rag_cite.py`'s AlignScore gate as a model acting as a check; that gate is in the measurement
harness of 2026-10-01, where it was found to add nothing, and `dawnr cite` does not use it.

"weeks" are the scouts' estimates. Cards on the lab are training until the evening of 2026-10-05, so the first

trained model; CRANE's result (constrain only the answer region, after free reasoning) explains why and is the
form to try for a prompted writer, whose answers mostly fail to parse.
