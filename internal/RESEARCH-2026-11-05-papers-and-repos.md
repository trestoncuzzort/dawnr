# What to take from other people's work: 250 papers, 250 repositories (sweep of 2026-11-05)

The operator, 2026-11-05: "ultracode for 250 papers and code from 250 repos we can take code from to help dawnr". The earlier
sweep is [RESEARCH-2026-10-05-what-to-steal.md](RESEARCH-2026-10-05-what-to-steal.md). The lists, field by field, the validator and its receipts are in
[`data/research-2026-11-05/`](data/research-2026-11-05/).

## How it was made, and how far to trust it

Two research agents, one per list, 644 tool calls and 83 minutes. Each was told what dawnr is, where it has measured itself weak, and the
rules: nothing from memory, code only under a licence dawnr can ship or train on, no entry whose sentence could describe another.
**Work dawnr had already cited did not count:** 1,209 arXiv ids and 1,069 repositories found in this repository's own record were excluded.

There was no reviewing agent. A script stands in for one: [`validate.py`](data/research-2026-11-05/validate.py), written before the agents started, and tested
first on 21 planted defects, all caught. It was rerun on the finished lists, independently of the agents' own runs:

| what it checked | papers | repositories |
|---|---:|---:|
| entries | 250 | 250 |
| problems | 0 | 0 |
| could not be checked | 0 | 0 |

For a paper, the id resolves to the stated title and year, and a quotation of 6 to 45 words really is in the abstract or full text. For a repository, it exists, is not a
fork, carries the stated licence (MIT, Apache-2.0, BSD, ISC, CC0 or CC-BY) as read from GitHub or from the licence file, and holds every listed path at the pinned commit.

**What that does not show.** It shows each entry is real and says what its source says. It does not show that a take would help dawnr, that the code fits dawnr's platforms,
or that a paper's claim is true. No entry here has been run, except the ones under "Taken already". What each source claims is its own claim on its own data. None of it is a
dawnr measurement.

What the agents themselves say is thin, in their words or close to them:

- **Floors were missed.** Repositories: proof back ends 25 of a target of 40, specification quality 13 of 20. Papers: specification quality 30 of 35, proof back ends 27 of 35,
  training 21 of 25, constrained generation 24 of 25. Nearly every strong paper and repository on the seven kernels is already in the list of excluded work. That says something about how much of this
  field dawnr has already read, and not only about the search.
- **Search channels failed.** OpenAlex's free budget ran out and it contributed nothing. Semantic Scholar answered 429 throughout, apart from citations of 9 seed papers. arXiv's API answered 429 for long
  stretches. SV-COMP and VerifyThis participant lists were not walked.
- **No paper has its code looked up.** So no paper entry says `steal-code`. This part of the task was left undone.
- **Tier A reading was partial in places.** Some appendices and results tables were not read, and repository code was read as heads or matching functions, not whole modules.
- Falsifiers on lower tiers are proposed tests, with thresholds an agent chose.
- 22 repositories worth learning from carry another licence, or none. They are in [`repos-other-licenses.jsonl`](data/research-2026-11-05/repos-other-licenses.jsonl) and are not to be copied from.
- Entries wrapping output from GPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, Llama or Gemma models say so. Their methods can be learned from, and their output is never training data.

One defect in the validator itself, found by an agent: its floors for specification quality and proof back ends were swapped. Floors only warn, so no result changed. Fixed here.

## What the lists suggest dawnr may be seeing wrongly

These are the agents' readings of the sources, with the ids behind them. **They are leads, not results.** Each has to be measured here first.

**1. "Repair needs a better error message" may be the wrong frame.** At matched budget, blind resampling matched or beat feedback repair for small models in placebo-controlled tests, and what helped was
information: the problem's specification or a solution. dawnr's own repair round moved the gate from 66 to 69 of 182 cells, which fits, but dawnr has never run a placebo or a resampling arm. What would change: the next repair
experiment becomes a factorial of resampling, a content-free placebo, the witness, and the specification. (arXiv:2607.26117, arXiv:2606.31511, arXiv:2610.28834, arXiv:2604.21950, arXiv:2610.22568)

**2. Agreement may carry less than it looks like.** Samples can collapse onto one wrong reading, on 10 to 16 percent of MBPP tasks in one study, invisibly without an oracle. Verifiers that share a source lose accuracy just where one
vote decides. So seven-way agreement may be worth fewer than seven votes, which `README.md` already warns about in words. What would change: report error coincidence per lowering, beside the conjunction.
(arXiv:2607.01953, arXiv:2609.26144, arXiv:2610.30059, arXiv:2608.21962; for checks of each lowering against one semantics, `runtimeverification/k`, `pereq-dev/hax`)

**3. Precision is partly a question of what is measured.** One study reports 99.1 percent verifier acceptance against 1.9 percent of certificates bound to a specification-defined predicate. dawnr already measures what that paper calls
fidelity, through tests, reference programs and mutants, so a binding check may add little here, and that needs measuring over the proved corpus. The 60 percent mutant threshold and the 0.682 precision figure may also move
with equivalent mutants and solver seeds. (arXiv:2611.05590, arXiv:2610.32053, arXiv:2607.08885, `YosysHQ/mcy`, `secure-foundations/mariposa`)

**4. Constraining the decoder fixes structure, not meaning, and masking changes the distribution.** Schema validity can reach 100 percent while content stays wrong. The 1.23 times gain dawnr measured changed masking and sample budget together, so it may
over- or understate masking. What would change: judge the type-and-scope pilot on newly covered problems, and compare masking with distribution-preserving samplers at equal sample counts.
(arXiv:2609.23742, arXiv:2610.35328, arXiv:2608.10137, arXiv:2605.07698, `large-loris-models/casa`)

**5. An agent's reading that this project's record refutes.** The list suggested that zero added rows from the JSON-v2 trial might be a deduplication problem, with the 142 converted replies repeating covered problems. [The trial's own result](../release/evidence/cohere-json-v2-pilot-result-2026-10-29.json)
says otherwise: of 500 requests, 356 were refused, 347 of them at static checking, 142 were accepted, and only 2 problems survived the strict check, both already covered. So at most 2 problems could have been duplicates, and the loss is the fall from 142 to 2.

A nearby question was cheap to measure, and was measured, without a prediction, so it is a description only. [`duplicates.py`](data/research-2026-11-05/duplicates.py) over `t/out/v5-full-20261011/train-rows.jsonl`, the bounded retrain mixture of 2026-10-11. **This is not the training set of the released model**, which has 5,095 rows,
hash `848fca29…`, and is not in this checkout, nor the 6,954-row candidate's, which is encrypted. Of its 3,352 rows there are 1,726 distinct targets once names are erased, so 48.5 percent of rows would go if each were kept once. Much of that is on purpose: 717 repair rows
share 188 targets, under 717 distinct prompts. Whether repeats help or hurt a small model has not been measured. (`bigcode-project/selfcodealign`, `MinishLab/semhash`, `lm-sys/llm-decontaminator`)

**6. Cases can start from known fixes.** Mining fix commits for changed functions gives the native gate a recall measurement it does not have, since a case's defect is then known in advance. (`Team-Atlanta/aixcc-asc-atlantis`, `theorem-labs/oss-verify`, `ispras/oss-sydr-fuzz`)

Chosen by what is measured weak and what can be run here without spending credit. This order is a proposal, and each needs its own prediction before it runs.
