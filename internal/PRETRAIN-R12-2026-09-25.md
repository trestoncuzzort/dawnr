# Re-pretraining the 92M core for the small-data regime (r12, track W5)

Written 2026-09-25. Prepared and tested on the lab's CPU; nothing here has run
on a GPU, and the sweep below waits for free cards. Every claim names the file
or the fetched page it came from; the research-first receipts are
7954a29d4881 (nanoGPT), 7b6dbe60cbce (the sweep's four papers) and
0304c0489396 (replay).

## Why

The core every locallm round continues from
(`t/out/source-pretraining-longer-2026-09-19/gpt-seed1337`) is 92,920,320
parameters trained for 4,000 steps of 65,536 tokens on a 48,798,892-token
train split: 262M tokens, 5.4 passes, at lr 3e-4 and weight decay 0.1 on every
tensor. That is a Chinchilla-shaped recipe applied to a corpus 40 times too
small for it. The literature for exactly this regime says two things
(`internal/research/r12-2026-09-21/training-lit.md`, items 5 and 6):

- **Repeat the data for many more epochs, but regularize much harder.**
  Kim et al. (arXiv:2509.14786, Figure 3) tuned a 150M model on 200M tokens to
  lr 3e-3, 16 epochs and weight decay 0.8, "over 30x larger than the standard
  practice of 0.1"; at 0.1 both more epochs and more parameters overfit (their
  1.4B lost to their 600M). Muennighoff et al. (arXiv:2305.16264) find four
  epochs as good as fresh data and returns past that still positive but
  decaying. Xu et al. (arXiv:2606.06888) build every 72M-1.4B data-constrained
  run on strong weight decay first. SmolLM2's 135M and 360M trained at lr 3e-3
  (arXiv:2502.02737, section 6).
- **Measure forgetting before curing it, then replay.** Ibrahim et al.
  (arXiv:2403.08763) replay 5% of the previous data under a weak shift and 25%
  under a strong one; MiniCPM (arXiv:2404.06395, Table 1) mixed its fine-tune
  data into the decay phase for MBPP 24.4 -> 30.3 while more fine-tune tokens
  alone did nothing.

## What changed in the code

1. **`train.make_optimizer(model, lr, weight_decay=0.1)`** now builds two AdamW
   groups: tensors with two or more dimensions under `weight_decay`, everything
   else (RMSNorm and LayerNorm gains, biases) under 0.0, exactly nanoGPT's
   `configure_optimizers` (raw.githubusercontent.com/karpathy/nanoGPT/master/model.py).
   The default stays 0.1 so every recorded command keeps its value. What does
   change for a *new* run at 0.1 is that norm gains and biases are no longer
   decayed; at 0.1 x 3e-4 x 4,000 steps that shrank a gain by under 10% over
   the whole core run, and at the sweep's 0.8 it would have been the
   difference between a model and a broken one. On the core's own
   configuration (measured on the lab CPU with `train.decay_split`) the split
   is 50 decayed tensors holding 92,798,976 parameters and 98 undecayed
   tensors holding 121,344 (every `ln_*.weight`, `ln_*.bias` and linear
   bias), 92,920,320 in all. Existing checkpoints are not touched; an
   optimizer state saved by the old single-group layout cannot be loaded into
   the new one, and `torch` says so rather than continuing.
2. **`train_distributed.py --weight-decay`**, recorded in the run identity under
   `optimizer` (kind, betas, weight_decay, the rule) and the split's counts under
   `optimizer_groups` in run.json; a resume with another value is refused
   ("Resume inputs changed: optimizer"). `run_pretraining_study.CONFIG` gains
   `weight_decay: 0.1`, so its commands now say what they always did, and
   `R12_SWEEP` holds the three arms below; `test_r12_pretrain_prep.py` checks
   the commands in this note against those entries.
3. **`continue_from_checkpoint.py --replay-data <train.txt> --replay-frac F`**
   draws `round(F x batch)` rows of every batch from the source corpus as
   block-size windows, exactly as pretraining drew them, on the windows path
   and under `--doc-batches` alike; a fraction that leaves no replay row or no
   target row is refused by name. **`--source-validation <validation.txt>`**
   (the file beside `--replay-data` by default, or alone) has the source
   corpus's loss measured on fixed windows (`train.EVAL_SEED`, the same windows
   before and after) and written to run.json as `source_losses`, so the
   no-replay control measures whether there was anything to forget. Replay
   text passes the same gates as `--data` (held-out ids under every alias, the
   same-task exclusions) plus the dev split, and is refused, never trimmed.
   The real source corpus passes: 0 problem ids named in its 149M characters.

## The sweep

Three arms, one seed each first, on the `gpt` architecture (the one the
current core uses; the `modern` arm lost at an untuned lr). The control keeps
the standard decay at the tuned learning rate, so the only difference between
it and the second arm is the decay. lr 1e-3 is also what `train.auto_lr`
gives width 768. Dropout stays at 0.0: the training-lit report (item 4) records
Xue et al. (arXiv:2305.13230) seeing decay on top of dropout diverge under
repeated data, and a sweep should change one regulariser.

| arm | lr | weight decay | steps | passes over the train split |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| wd0.8-lr1e-3 | 1e-3 | 0.8 | 11,200 | 15.0 |
| wd0.8-lr3e-3 | 3e-3 | 0.8 | 11,200 | 15.0 |
| wd0.1-lr3e-3-control | 3e-3 | 0.1 | 11,200 | 15.0 |

11,200 steps x 65,536 tokens = 734M tokens = 15.0 passes over 48,798,892
tokens (the research note's "46M tokens, 16 epochs" was rounded; 16 exact
passes would be 11,914 steps, and the plan's 11,200 is kept). Warm-up is
steps / 20 = 560, as the study's own rule.

### What it costs, and what it waits for

- **Time.** The recorded `gpt` arm took 961.4 s wall (864.6 s in the
  optimizer) for 4,000 updates on four cards
  (`t/out/source-pretraining-longer-2026-09-19/gpt-seed1337/run.json`), so
  11,200 updates is 2,692 s, about 45 minutes per arm; three arms, one seed,
  2.2 hours; three seeds per arm, 6.7 hours. The `modern` arm ran 1,141 s per
  4,000, 53 minutes per arm, and is not in the sweep.
- **Memory.** The `gpt` arm's peak was 8.88 GB allocated and 9.81 GB reserved
  per rank (the same run.json); `run_pretraining_study.MIN_FREE_MIB` asks for
  13 GiB free per card, measured on the `modern` arm's 11.90 GiB reservation.
- **The cards, on 2026-09-25 at 20:10 local time** (`nvidia-smi`, read only):
  the lab's four RTX 6000 Ada (49,140 MiB each) had 5,053 / 6,837 / 7,404 /
  6,679 MiB free, the rest held by another user's eight `llama-server`
  processes at roughly 41-43 GB per card. No arm fits; the sweep waits until
  a card shows at least 13 GiB free, and `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES` names the free
  ones. Those GPUs are off limits until then; nothing in this track uses one.

### What decides

Not loss: validation loss has twice failed to predict behaviour here
(`t/RUN-NEXT-locallm-r12.md`). Each arm's `ckpt.pt` is a core that
`continue_from_checkpoint.py --init` reads as it reads today's; run the
section-C fine-tune on each, decode the dev split, and count tests passed
(LIMA and phi-1 chose checkpoints by generation quality, receipt 63eda36902d8
in the training-lit report). Report the arm's source validation loss beside
it as the secondary number, never as the verdict. Predictions go in a
`t/PREDICT-*` file before the first arm trains.

## Replay in the r12 fine-tune

The section-C command with replay, and its control (the same run, measuring
forgetting with no replay rows):

```bash
~/.venv-vllm/bin/python locallm/continue_from_checkpoint.py \
    --init <core> --data t/out/loop/corpus-r12-headed.txt --split t/out/loop/split-v5.json \
    --out t/out/locallm-r12-replay-s<seed> --steps 300 --lr 3e-5 --block-size 512 \
    --doc-batches --keep-every 50 --dropout 0.1 --split-seed 1337 --seed <seed> --deterministic \
    --replay-data t/out/source-corpus-2026-09-19-v2/train.txt --replay-frac 0.25

~/.venv-vllm/bin/python locallm/continue_from_checkpoint.py \
    --init <core> --data t/out/loop/corpus-r12-headed.txt --split t/out/loop/split-v5.json \
    --out t/out/locallm-r12-s<seed> --steps 300 --lr 3e-5 --block-size 512 \
    --doc-batches --keep-every 50 --dropout 0.1 --split-seed 1337 --seed <seed> --deterministic \
    --source-validation t/out/source-corpus-2026-09-19-v2/validation.txt
```

At batch 8 and fraction 0.25 every step trains on 6 document rows and 2
replay windows. `run.json` records under `identities.replay` the file and its
sha256, the fraction, `rows_per_batch` 2, `target_rows_per_batch` 6,
`row_share` 0.25 and `token_share_estimate`; under `identities.source_validation`
the validation file, its sha256, the window count and the evaluation seed; and
under `source_losses.before` and `.after` the source corpus's loss in nats per
token on those fixed windows. A resume with another replay file or fraction
is refused.

**Rows are not tokens under `--doc-batches`.** A document row is padded past
its real tokens while a replay window is all 512, so two replay rows carry
more of the loss than a quarter; the run record states the estimate
(`token_share_estimate`, computed from the corpus's mean real tokens per
document) instead of pretending the flag is the share. Measured on
`t/out/loop/corpus-r8-headed.txt` with the core's tokenizer: 301 documents,
182.6 target tokens each on average, one cut at 512, so two replay rows in
eight carry **48.3%** of the loss tokens at `--replay-frac 0.25`. On the
windows path the two are equal. Whether to lower `--replay-frac` for document
batches (0.1 gives one replay row in ten, about 22% of the tokens) is a
decision for the run, made with that number in view, not a default.

**First measurement on the real core** (lab CPU, eight threads, niced; two
updates of the windows path at lr 3e-5 warming up from 1.5e-6, batch 8, block
512, `--replay-frac 0.25`; the target corpus was the r8 headed corpus with the
36 documents the gates refuse removed; output under a scratch directory, not
`t/out`). Before any update the core's loss on 32 fixed 512-token windows of
the source corpus was **1.259 nats/token on its train split and 1.670 on its
validation split**; after two updates 1.259 and 1.665. Two things to read off
it. The path works end to end on the real corpus and the frozen tokenizer
(two replay rows and six target rows per step, token share 0.25 on the
windows path, the whole run about seven minutes of which 90 s is tokenizing
without `LOCALLM_TOKEN_CACHE`). And the 1.259 / 1.670 gap says the core
already fits its own train split far better than held-out source at 512
tokens of context (the study's record has 1.146 / 1.268 at 2,048), so
forgetting has to be read on both splits: replay rows come from `train.txt`
and protect the first number directly, while the second says whether the
core still generalises over source. Two updates at 1e-6 move nothing, as they
should; the number the r12 run needs is the 300-step one.

## What was not done

- No arm has trained; no card was free. The note is the recipe, not a result.
- The real core's source losses are measured over two updates only (above);
  the 300-step before and after, the number that says whether the fine-tune
  forgets, comes with the first r12 seed.
- `continue_from_checkpoint.py --data` is not checked against the dev split
  (the builder and preflight are); replay data is. Out of this track's scope,
  listed for the caller.
- The 300M arm (Kim: wd 1.6, roughly 7 hours on four cards) waits for this
  sweep's answer, as the training-lit report ranks it.
