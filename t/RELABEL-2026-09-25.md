### Train problems that can never be a target: their points cannot reject a lazy program

AlphaVerus's exploit model (arXiv:2412.06176) applied to the problem's own tests: when a constant, the identity, the input's length or its reversal answers every recorded point, the points cannot tell a solution from the laziest program, so no relabel onto that problem is evidence of anything. The first run of 2026-09-25 admitted 26 of its 131 rows onto 6 such problems before this refusal existed, among them `r := n` for "find the nth digit of 1, 2, 3, ..." whose one recorded point is `[3] -> 3`; the reference check did not catch it because the draws copy the example's shape (0 to 6, where the nth digit is n).

This also refuses problems whose function IS the lazy program (the larger of two numbers, x + 1): a correct relabel onto them is lost on purpose, because the evidence that would admit it is the evidence that admitted `r := n` above, and a lost right row costs less than an added wrong one. The lazy programs are spec_check.lazy_functions, unchanged.

## How the rows are used, and the weighting they still owe

`python3 t/loop_dataset.py --from-samples ... --relabel-rows <this file>` appends the rows to the positives with `source: relabel` kept, through the same refusals as every other row (held-out under every alias, the same-task exclusions, the dev split: a row naming any of them stops the build, it is not dropped). The corpus builder then writes each one under the target problem's own `Problem:`/`Signature:` head.

Nothing weights them yet. CodeIt's learning stage samples real solutions more often than relabeled ones (priority proportional to the share of demonstration outputs the program got right); its ablation A3, uniform sampling over the same buffer, fell from 49/400 to 38/400 on policy performance, "indicating that the policy indeed forgets important experiences" (https://arxiv.org/html/2402.04858, Table 2). The source field is what a later builder needs to weight real positives higher; until one does, a relabeled row counts exactly as much as a real one in the corpus.

## What was learned

- 2421 wrong-but-proven rows are 595 distinct programs: the population is mostly the same attractors recited by many arms, 4.1 copies each on average.
- 123 of 595 distinct programs solve exactly one train problem (21 percent), over 43 target problems; 87 solve two or more (the same function under several problem statements) and 385 solve none.
- Of 120218 (program, candidate) pairs, 119406 fail the candidate's points, 401 pass every point and then disagree with the reference on a draw: a program that passes three assertions is not yet a program that computes the function, and the reference check is not decorative.
- 249 train candidates were refused because a lazy program answers every recorded point; a relabel onto them would have been a row about nothing.
- Two instruments were too weak on the first two runs and were tightened before any row was used: a target whose points a lazy program answers admitted `r := n` for the nth digit, and draws shaped like the first example alone admitted a lookup table for happy numbers. Both are properties of the evidence, not of the programs, and both are now refused by name.

## What this differs from CodeIt in, and why

CodeIt relabels to the realized output, so every valid program becomes data. Here the target must be an existing train problem, must be solved on every point, and the program's own specification must agree with that problem's reference solution on random draws and at its stated examples. The yield is therefore a small fraction of the population, and each admitted row carries a real problem's English, which is the thing locallm has to learn to read (internal/RESEARCH-NEXT-2026-09-20.md). A program that qualifies for two problems is not admitted for either.
