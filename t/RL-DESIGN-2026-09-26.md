# Reinforcement learning for locallm, with the proof engine as the reward: design, inspection, feasibility, decision

2026-09-26. The dawnr row this serves: "get better at reasoning: reinforcement
learning with the verifier as the reward" (`AMBITION.md`). Code: `t/rl_reward.py`
(the reward), `t/rl_feasibility.py` (the measurement below), `t/rl_grpo.py` (the
trainer), `t/rl_spec_pool.py` (the specification-only prompt pool). Tests:
`t/test_rl_reward.py`, `t/test_rl_grpo.py`. Outputs under
`t/out/rl-2026-09-26/` (not committed).

## 1. The policy: r11 seed 8

The strongest locallm checkpoint on record is **`locallm-r11-s8`** (lab
`t/out/locallm-r11-s8`, ckpt sha256 `71da09f1…`): a 92.9M GPT, BPE 8,192,
block 512 in training, fine-tuned 300 steps at lr 3e-5 from the
`source-pretraining-longer` core on `corpus-r8-headed.txt` (49,890 training
tokens, 79 headed problem documents). On
`t/FINDINGS-r11-baseline-2026-09-26.md` it leads every column: 7 tests-pass
and 5 clean of 232 (tied on clean with s4), and the only seed that passes any
of the clean 200 (2). That lead is inside what nine seeds of one recipe draw
(Dodge's expected best of nine is 1.31), so it is chosen as the best
available draw, not as a better recipe. The desktop's `model-r4`, `model-r5`
and the r12 pretraining arms are older or not fine-tuned; nothing r12 has
been trained yet.

## 2. The reward, tiered

One answer walks the road a held-out answer walks, calling the existing code:
`spec_experiment.find_block`, `surface.parse`, `rename_task`,
`fuzz_lower.check_wf`, `spec_experiment.run_point` over the problem's points,
`spec_check.check_task` / `check_points` / `exploit`, and `run_par.py
--kernels dafny` on the lab. The reward is the furthest tier reached:

| tier | reward | what it takes |
|---|---:|---|
| none | 0.00 | no t block, or it does not parse |
| parses | 0.05 | parses, `check_wf` rejects it |
| typed | 0.10 | well formed (t's type check) |
| tests | 0.50 | every one of the problem's assertions passes, **and no drawn input separates it from the problem's reference solution** |
| proved-weak | 0.75 | tests, Dafny `verified / refuted` (real proved, twin refuted), the spec not contradicted by the reference on 50 draws or by the problem's examples, no lazy program satisfies it, **but a mutated wrong output also satisfies it** |
| proved | 1.00 | the same, with a spec no mutated output satisfies |

Composite rule-based rewards follow DeepSeek-R1 (arXiv:2501.12948: rule
rewards, a format part and an accuracy part, no learned reward model); GRPO
normalises inside a group (arXiv:2402.03300 section 4.1.2), so the order of
the tiers matters more than their values. Rewards are cached by sha256 of
(task id, extracted t block), versioned (`REWARD_VERSION`), under
run_par's own verdict cache on the lab.

**The in-loop proof is Dafny alone**, all seven reserved for evaluation.
Why: Dafny is the fastest kernel, and on the 12 r11/r9 locallm answer sets
graded in all seven (3,066 rows), a row `verified / refuted` in Dafny is so in
all seven 2,210 of 2,260 times (97.8 percent), and no row is clean in all seven
without Dafny (0 of 806). Dafny is asked only for answers that already pass
their tests and whose spec is not already shown wrong (about 200 distinct
answers of 5,952 in the feasibility run); a batch of those grades in 10 to 30
seconds at `--jobs 2` (about 12 prover processes, inside the 16-core budget).

### 2.1 The inspection gate

99 answers from the policy on training problems (temperature 0.8), stratified
over the tiers (12 none, 12 parses, 20 typed, all 32 distinct test-passing
answers that did not prove, 23 proved), were read by hand against the problem,
its assertions, the grader's signals and the proof cell.
(`t/out/rl-2026-09-26/inspection.txt`.)

| tier read | answers | reward agrees with a careful reading | disagreements |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| none | 12 | 12 | 0: every one is garbled syntax (dots after expressions, two `decreases`, stray backticks, names with spaces) |
| parses | 12 | 12 | 0: each fails a real type rule (seq assigned to int, `len` of an int, `decreases` without recursion) |
| typed | 20 | 20 | 0: every one computes the wrong function (total instead of lateral surface, `n*(3n-3)` for nonagonal numbers, first instead of last occurrence) |
| tests (did not prove) | 32 | 23 | **9 wrong programs pass the problem's three assertions**: `n % 2 == 0` for "has 28 days" (2) and for "even parity" (1), wrong sets of 30-day and 31-day months (4), a min-of-three that misses one ordering (1), a last-occurrence search that returns `s[0]` instead of -1 when the value is absent (1). They scored 0.5, like a right program with a wrong spec. |
| proved | 23 | 22 | **1 proof of an incomplete spec**: min-of-three with `ensures r <= a; ensures r <= b; ensures r == a or r == b or r == c` (no `r <= c`; `(5, 6, 1)` admits 5). Scored 1.0, like a full spec. |

The 23 test-tier answers that agree are right programs whose spec is wrong
(`ensures 2 * r == 4 * d` for a perimeter of `4 * d`; Fibonacci's spec function
with the wrong base case), too weak (`r == a or r == b` for max, caught by the
lazy-program check), or right but unproved (a missing loop invariant; a
recursive call that violates its own `requires n >= 2`); 0.5 is right for all
of them. Two observations that are not disagreements: `spec_check` passed an
isosceles spec whose second `ensures` contradicts the first only when
`b == c != a`, which 50 random draws never hit; Dafny refuted it. And several
answers name another problem's id in the task name (`mbpp_728__small_nnum` for
problem 10); the grader renames every answer, so this costs nothing.

**The fixes.** (1) *Drawn-input tests*: the program is run against the
problem's reference solution on 50 inputs drawn like its examples
(`spec_check.draw`), EvalPlus-style differential testing
(arXiv:2305.01210); a wrong value or a crash drops the answer to `typed`. The
reference is trusted only after it reproduces every one of the problem's own
assertions when called the same way, so a string problem whose Python wants a
`str` is skipped rather than scored. (2) *proved-weak at 0.75*: a proof whose
spec also admits a mutated output (`spec_check`'s `weak`) sits between tests
and a full proof. After the fixes the whole feasibility set was re-graded: the
drawn tests moved exactly 9 answers to `typed` and all 9 are the wrong
programs above (each read again with its witness, e.g. `check_monthnum_number(4)`
answered true); 176 test-passing answers passed the drawn tests; the 4
proved-weak answers are the min-of-three above and three max-of-two proofs of
`r >= x; r >= y` (any upper bound). No disagreement remains in the 99, and no
new one was introduced among the 185 test-passing answers.

## 3. Feasibility

All 372 RL prompts (the only training problems pool v5 holds with test points
on this desktop: 287 MBPP and 85 HumanEval; split-v5's 2,266 APPS train ids
are not in the desktop's pool and the policy has never seen APPS-style
answers), 16 samples each, temperature 0.8, top-k 20, 1,200 tokens, the
prompt, stop and extraction of `loop_locallm.py generate`. RL prompts exclude
the split's 232 eval ids, the 100 dev ids and the 82 decontamination
exclusions. 5,952 answers; per sample: none 2,140, parses 1,711, typed 1,882,
tests 24, proved-weak 4, proved 191.

| tier (or better) | problems with any sample | fraction | groups with mixed outcomes | fraction | sample rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| parses | 372 | 1.000 | 362 | 0.973 | 0.641 |
| typed | 345 | 0.927 | 343 | 0.922 | 0.353 |
| **tests** | **38** | **0.102** | **38** | **0.102** | 0.037 |
| proved-weak | 34 | 0.091 | 34 | 0.091 | 0.033 |
| proved | 33 | 0.089 | 33 | 0.089 | 0.032 |

Under the composite reward every one of the 372 groups has nonzero variance,
but for 334 of them the variance is only between malformed and well-formed
wrong answers.

**Where the test-passing groups are.** 64 of the 372 prompts are problems
whose verified answer is in the policy's own fine-tuning corpus (named in
`corpus-r8-headed.txt` or with the same problem text). Of the 38 prompts with
any test-passing sample, **36 are those 64** (36 of 64, 56 percent; 217 of
their 1,024 samples pass); on the 308 problems the policy was not trained on,
**2 of 308** (0.6 percent: problem 204, count a character, right program with
a wrong spec; and HumanEval 60, sum to n, proved), 2 samples of 4,928.

## 4. The decision

The rule: stop if fewer than about 10 percent of groups have any sample at
the tests tier. The measurement is **10.2 percent**, so the rule is met, by
two problems, and only because of recitation: without the 64 memorised
problems the rate is 0.6 percent. RL on this policy and this pool can sharpen
what it has memorised (pass@16 into pass@1 on 36 problems it was trained on)
and has almost nothing to work with on problems it was not. That is the
reading the literature predicts for RL with verifiable rewards: Yue et al.
(arXiv:2504.13837, NeurIPS 2025) find RLVR-trained models beat their base at
pass@1 while the base wins at large k, the solved set bounded by the base
model's coverage, and that distillation, not RLVR, adds new patterns
(research receipt fda423591f0d). It reweights answers the base can already
sample; it does not create them.

So Phase 2 is run as a **pilot of the machinery and a test of that
prediction**, not as a step expected to improve reasoning, and the next step
recommended (section 7) is supervised, whatever the pilot shows.

## 5. The pilot, pre-registered before it ran

Written before the first pilot step; the numbers that falsify each prediction
are stated. Configuration: `t/rl_grpo.py`, 64 prompts
(`t/out/rl-2026-09-26/pilot-ids.json`: 32 drawn from the 64 in-corpus
prompts, 32 from the 308 others, seed 2026), 8 prompts a step, G = 16,
40 steps (5 passes over the 64), lr 5e-6, beta 0.04, clip 0.2, mu 1,
temperature 0.8, top-k 20, sequences capped at 512 tokens, Dafny on the lab.
Baseline from the feasibility samples of the same 64 prompts: in-corpus half
mean reward 0.295 and test-passing sample rate 0.260; the other half 0.041
and 0.000.

- **P1, the machinery holds.** The KL to the starting policy stays under
  0.05 nats per token at every step. Falsified if any step exceeds 0.1.
- **P2, GRPO sharpens what is memorised.** Mean reward over the in-corpus
  half in the last pass (steps 33-40) is at least 0.05 above the first pass
  (steps 1-8). Falsified if the gain is under 0.02.
- **P3, it does not create what is absent.** The test-passing sample rate on
  the not-in-corpus half stays at or under 2 percent in every pass.
  Falsified if the last pass exceeds 5 percent.
- **P4, format is the easiest signal.** The fraction of all samples at
  `parses` or better rises by at least 0.05 from the first pass to the last.
  Falsified if it does not rise at all.

Held-out problems are not generated on, graded, or evaluated in this task.

### 5.1 Pilot A, the result: two of four predictions falsified

`t/out/rl-2026-09-26/grpo-pilot/` (log.jsonl per step, steps/ with every
answer and its reward). 40 steps, about 25 s a step (1.7 s sampling, the rest
grading and the update). Per pass over the 64 prompts:

| pass (steps) | in-corpus reward | in-corpus tests+ rate | other reward | other tests+ rate | all parses+ rate | max KL |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 (1-8) | 0.240 | 0.195 | 0.052 | 0.000 | 0.783 | 0.057 |
| 2 (9-16) | 0.198 | 0.137 | 0.064 | 0.000 | 0.810 | 0.143 |
| 3 (17-24) | 0.171 | 0.113 | 0.044 | 0.000 | 0.607 | 0.758 |
| 4 (25-32) | 0.136 | 0.066 | 0.066 | 0.000 | 0.723 | 0.652 |
| 5 (33-40) | 0.165 | 0.082 | 0.088 | 0.000 | 0.899 | 0.472 |

KL per token by step: 0.003 (2), 0.049 (7), 0.143 (14), 0.320 (19), 0.758
(24, the peak), 0.287 (40). No ratio was ever clipped (mu = 1, so the ratio is
1 at the update by construction).

- **P1 falsified**: KL passed 0.1 at step 14 and peaked at 0.76.
- **P2 falsified, in the wrong direction**: the in-corpus reward fell from
  0.240 to 0.165 and its test-passing rate from 19.5 to 8.2 percent. GRPO
  unlearned memorised correct answers.
- **P3 held**: 0 test-passing samples on the other half in every pass.
- **P4 held**: well-formed-or-better rose from 0.783 to 0.899; the other
  half's reward rose from 0.052 to 0.088, all of it format.

**Why.** Most groups have no test-passing answer, so their only spread is 0 /
0.05 / 0.10. GRPO divides each group by its own standard deviation, which
turns a 0.05 format step into an advantage the size of a proof (about ±1).
With 5 or 6 of every 8 groups like that, the update was mostly "parse
better", paid for with the memorised programs: the illusory advantages of
minimal score differences that Pref-GRPO names (arXiv:2508.20751), and one of
the normalization biases Dr. GRPO removes (arXiv:2503.20783; research receipt
ecf12bfac667). What it bought: the tiers themselves are sound, but their
spacing is only safe if the advantage keeps it.

### 5.2 Pilot B, registered before it ran: Dr. GRPO advantages

Everything as pilot A except `--advantage mean` (advantage r - mean, no
division by the group's std; `t/rl_grpo.group_advantages`), so a 0.05 format
step stays 0.05 and a proof stays about 0.5 to 0.9.

- **P1'**: KL under 0.1 nats per token at every step. Falsified if any step
  exceeds 0.2.
- **P2'**: the in-corpus reward in the last pass is not below the first pass
  by more than 0.02 (no unlearning). Falsified if it falls by 0.05 or more.
  (A rise of 0.05 would be the sharpening pilot A was expected to show; not
  predicted with confidence at lr 5e-6 and 40 steps, because advantages are
  now about ten times smaller for format-only groups and no larger for the
  others.)
- **P3'**: the other half's test-passing rate stays at or under 2 percent.

## 6. The specification-only pool (vericoding)

Scope added by the operator: vericoding-benchmark's specification-only Dafny
tasks as a second prompt pool, whose reward is the verifier alone and whose
verified samples become training documents (expert iteration).

**Source.** `t/out/L1/vericoding-benchmark` (MIT): 2,334 Dafny
specifications (`specs/DA*_specs.dfy`, body `assume {:axiom} false;`, English
in `jsonl/dafny_tasks.jsonl`). 1,754 of them have an AI-written verified
solution in `vericoded/`, which the lift of 2026-09-26 already used as a
source of training documents (`t/LIFT-2026-09-26.md`); 580 have none. As
prompts all 2,334 are usable by the current policy (it never trained on
either); once r12's corpus includes the lifted solutions, those specs become
recitation for the next policy and should leave the pool or be reported
apart.

**Spec to t prompt** (`t/rl_spec_pool.py`). The lifter lifts only a method
with a body, so the staged copy replaces `assume false` by a stub that assigns
each return a default of its type and nothing else; the lifter then lifts the
declaration, clauses and every predicate and function the clauses use, as it
does for a verified program, refusing by name what t cannot express. The stub
never reaches a prompt: the prompt is `Problem:` (the task's English),
`Signature:`, then the lifted task printed up to its body's opening brace,
which is exactly where every program document in the corpus continues with a
body. The answer is that prefix plus the model's body, so the model cannot
change the spec, and the reward is the proof alone: 0 unparseable, 0.05
parses, 0.10 well formed, 1.0 Dafny `verified / refuted`. The twin still
guards against a trivially weak spec (a `verified / decorative` cell is not a
proof).

**Gates** (before a spec is asked; `rl_spec_pool.py gate`):
1. the lifted spec must be well formed in t (`check_wf`): the lift ran
   without its kernel check, and some lifted specs are ill-typed (a
   seq-valued function lifted as int);
2. held-out ids, dev ids and same-task exclusions under every alias in the
   task and its vericoding row (`loop_filter.TrainingDataGate` over the
   merged policy, `t/r12-dev-ids.json`); vericoding's `humaneval` source names
   `humaneval_NNN`, and its `dafnybench` source can carry MBPP-derived
   `dafny_synthesis_task_id_N` files, both of which alias pool ids;
3. a *specification twin*: the spec's `ensures`, with a gated problem's own
   expected output bound to the result, holds at all of that problem's test
   points, and the problem's reference on 100 drawn inputs never contradicts
   it; vericoding numbers APPS its own way (0 of 13 in-pool ids matched by
   text on 2026-09-26), so ids alone cannot be trusted. Points alone
   over-refuse (a smoke run "twinned" a champagne-pyramid spec with binomial
   coefficients); the draws remove such coincidences, and a problem with no
   runnable reference keeps the refusal.
4. A sample that verifies is gated again **as a program** before it becomes a
   training document: `lift_corpora`'s behavioural twin on 100 drawn inputs
   against every gated problem.

**Counts** (full stage and gate: see 6.1). **Measured success on a sample**:
see 6.1.

**How it fits the literature** (`/home/t/resources/STEAL.md`, items 7 and 9).
PSV (Propose, Solve, Verify, arXiv:2512.18160, read in full for its method,
research receipt 1e6c029465a8) is the reference design: a solver trained by
rejection fine-tuning on verifier-accepted solutions, a proposer that makes
new specs, and difficulty-aware proposal from the solver's pass rates; its
ablations name verification and difficulty-awareness as essential. Here the
proposer's role is played first by the fixed lifted vericoding pool (a 92M
model cannot propose specs), later by the prompted 27B; difficulty-awareness
becomes the STP-style band already in `t/LITERATURE-2026-09.md` (keep specs
whose empirical pass rate is in (0, 1/4]). SAFE (arXiv:2410.15756) and STaR
(arXiv:2203.14465) say failed attempts are data: every sample that is well
formed but does not verify is kept with Dafny's error text as a (spec, wrong
body, verifier message) record, the input half of a self-debugging triple,
whose output half is the verified body when a later sample or the teacher
produces one. CodeIt-style hindsight relabeling already exists here
(`t/relabel.py`, `t/RELABEL-2026-09-25.md`: 118 programs admitted for the
train problem they actually solve) and is reused, not rebuilt: a verified
body that fails its intended spec's tests but solves another train problem
goes through `relabel.py`'s gates unchanged. Absolute-Zero-style
self-proposal is not adopted alone: a model rewarded by its own verifier on
tasks it proposes learns to propose easy ones (STEAL.md, cautions).
