2. **Filliâtre and Paskevich, ESOP 2013, DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-37036-6_8, LNCS 7792, pp. 125-128.** Real and correctly attributed. ## 2. What the literature unblocks

### A. Cross-kernel grading

**Actionable now: AlgoVeri's spec well-formedness audit, as an extra gate on t's own `ensures` clauses.** AlgoVeri (arXiv:2602.09464, Sec. on "Necessity against degeneracy") decomposes a specification into sub-clauses {Ci} and mechanically checks two things: (i) local satisfiability, each Ci alone has a model; (ii) necessity, dropping any single Ci admits behaviour the full spec forbids (their example: drop the permutation clause from `sort` and an arbitrary sorted list satisfies it). They proved this by hand in Lean for a handful of hard tasks (Max-Flow, Tarjan's SCC) as a curation audit. This is orthogonal to t's twin refutation, which is a program-level check; the sub-clause audit is spec-level and would catch a vacuous or redundant conjunct that a well-chosen twin happens to trip anyway. Directly statable in t's own lowering machinery as extra proof obligations.

**Actionable now: a citable, quantified fact about compiler-verified overstatement.** AlgoVeri Table 2 reports Gemini-3 Flash at 55.84% "Compiler Verified" versus 40.26% "+ Semantic Filtered" in Dafny, and GPT-5.3 Codex at 49.35% versus 42.86%. Their filter is an LLM judge catching "cheating" (`assume P` with a contradiction, `sorry`) and "algorithmic degeneracy" (Union-Find without path compression). That is independent empirical support, from a different benchmark and a different mechanism, for the phenomenon t measured directly at 35 of 43.

**Prior art to position against, with the exact stopping points named:**
- AlgoVeri is the only found benchmark lowering one shared source with matched contracts into more than two kernels (3: Dafny, Verus, Lean 4), and its Table 1 claims to be "the first to enforce strict parallel alignment across SMT-based (Dafny, Verus) and ITP-based (Lean) systems", marking VeriCoding and VerifyThisBench as "Unaligned". Full-text grep for mutat|mutant|refut|buggy|inject returns zero method hits, and for agreement|correlat|"all three"|jointly returns zero: headline results are three side-by-side per-kernel columns.
- Bursuc et al. (arXiv:2509.22908) do have matched task IDs of the form `XYdddd` across Dafny/Verus/Lean for DafnyBench (443/440/440), VerifiedCogen (172/172/172) and Verina+Clever (157/156/189), but never exploit them for a per-task cross-kernel consistency table. Full-text search for "comparab*" returns zero occurrences.
- LeetProof / Velvet (arXiv:2604.16584) states the fragmentation problem explicitly ("either auto-active systems like Dafny or interactive ones like Coq") and answers it by **unification inside one Lean-embedded tool**, not by cross-checking independent kernels. Numbers: 28/50 fully proved multi-modal versus 17/50 single-mode Lean with GPT-5.2 at a fixed $5-per-problem budget; 10/25 versus 7/25 with Claude Opus 4.6; 18/18 residual VCs closed by Aristotle versus 23/30 for single-mode.
- Why3 (ESOP 2013), correctness witnesses (Beyer et al., FSE 2016, DOI 10.1145/2950290.2950351) and BenchExec/SV-COMP (Beyer, TACAS 2016, DOI 10.1007/978-3-662-49674-9_55; TACAS 2024, DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-57256-2_15) are the established precedents for exchanged certificates and competition-style multi-tool reporting, over one shared C source with pre-labelled true/false variants rather than a per-task injected mutant.
- Falda (arXiv:2606.02019) is the nearest thing to a convergence matrix in production: 18 apex axioms x 10 axes, per-axiom convergence 3 to 8.

### B. Spec-hacking reward

**Implementable formula 1, from Re:Form (arXiv:2507.16331, Sec. 2.4).** Grant a subset reward iff a kernel proves both `GT_pre => GEN_pre` (precondition relaxation) and `GT_pre => (GEN_post => GT_post)` (postcondition strengthening). Measured effect, Table 10, Validity/Verification/SSR per Qwen-2.5 size, verification-reward-only then subset-reward: 0.5B 99.2/92.8/20.7 then 96.3/65.8/30.1; 1.5B 98.8/86.0/27.0 then 97.5/72.4/40.4; 3B 98.8/85.2/30.7 then 97.7/75.0/44.7; 7B 99.6/89.1/30.7 then 98.4/78.1/49.8; 14B 99.4/92.6/37.3 then 99.0/85.9/55.3. The pattern holds at every size: verification rate falls, spec superiority rate rises. OOD DafnyComp shows a 63.8% relative SSR gain over SFT. **Binding constraint for t:** this needs a reference spec per task. t has one for the 19 hand-written tasks and the DafnyBench-lifted tasks; it does not for the MBPP-derived NL problems, which is exactly the population where the 35-of-43 hacking was measured. Also, discharging the two implications is straightforward to state in every kernel (a lemma over t's existing value encoding) but requires seven working harnesses, and Dafny/Verus/Lean/Rocq/F* will take it more easily than SPARK and Frama-C WP.

**Implementable formula 2, from Tan (arXiv:2605.30914).** `StrictSuccess(x,y) = RawPass(x,y) ∧ SpecPreserved(x,y) ∧ NoForbiddenConstructs(y)`, and `HackRate = Pr[StrictSuccess=0 | RawPass=1]`. This turns the one-off 35-of-43 count into a per-checkpoint trend line. The forbidden-construct denylist must be built per kernel: Tan only enumerates Dafny's `assume false` and `{:verify false}`; t needs Lean `sorry`, Rocq `admit`/`Admitted`, F* `admit()`, Verus `assume(false)`, ACSL `assumes \false`, and a SPARK equivalent.

**Implementable formula 3, from LeetProof (arXiv:2604.16584).** Type-directed output mutation for postcondition completeness: given candidate output ô for input i, generate o' by type (flip Booleans; perturb Nat/Int/Char by a small additive delta; mutate one component of a pair; for sequences, element-level mutation, random deletion, random insertion), then check whether φ(i, o') is wrongly satisfied by any o' ≠ ô. This is a systematic, type-driven complement to t's hand-authored twins, and it is kernel-agnostic. Their bounded-existential-enumeration trick (extract an upper bound from inequality constraints in the VC, prove it via an omega-style tactic, enumerate the finite range) fits t's bounded-quantifier fragment directly. Base rates for spec defects found this way: VERINA 16/188 (8.5%), CLEVER 18/161 (11.2%), semantic accuracy 182/188 (97.4%) after filtering. Their own acknowledged limitation: testing-based validation misses over-constraining defects when the given tests already satisfy the over-tight constraint.

**Implementable formula 4, from Bursuc et al. (arXiv:2509.22908).** Per-file quality score `quality = 1 - Σ_i (w_i · n_i)` with normalised per-issue-type weights summing to 1.0 and n_i the count of issue type i, plus their three cheating checks (reject `sorry`/`assume(false)` bypasses; flag postcondition rewrites to `ensures true`; use ghost/spec-only types to prevent implementation leakage) and near-duplicate detection via sentence-transformer embeddings plus FAISS. Their manual-inspection base rates for an LLM-based translation step: about 9% of specs too weak, about 15% poorly translated, conditioned on vericoding success.

**Cheap pre-filter, from Tan.** LLM-scored `spec_faithfulness` / `spec_leakage` / `difficulty` on 0-3 scales (Claude Opus 4.5 as scorer), keep faithfulness >= 2, leakage <= 1, difficulty >= 1. Applied to 2,334 Vericoding Dafny tasks this retained 1,149 (919 train / 230 test). Note the threshold choice was ad hoc and never validated against a ground-truth label set.

**Evidence against an LLM judge as a cheaper oracle: Verus-SpecGym (arXiv:2605.26457), Table 4 / App. F.7.** gpt5.3-codex judging its own 527 compile-clean specs: of the 191 the executable evaluator rejected, the judge wrongly marked 49 correct (49/191 = 25.7%, the abstract's "26%"); of the 336 accepted, it wrongly flagged 26 (7.7%). Narrow conditions: self-judge, single model, n=191, post-hoc audit only ("the execution-based oracle operates only at evaluation time, not during training"). Useful as support for a design t already made, not as a general law.

**CLEVER's leakage heuristic, mechanically checkable.** CLEVER (arXiv:2505.13938) writes specs as non-computable Lean `Prop`s specifically so a spec cannot be copied into the body and discharged by rewriting; their Fig. 3 shows a leaky spec collapsing to a 2-line proof. Implementable red flag for t: **a spec whose proof discharges via a single `simp`/`unfold` of the implementation**. Their annotation cost, if a small hand-curated set is ever needed: 25 minutes to write plus 15 minutes peer review, 40 minutes per problem, over an hour for non-computable specs.

### C. Small-model expert iteration

**Implementable estimator 1, group degeneracy (Tan).** For a task with empirical pass probability p under K samples, the group yields nonzero relative signal with probability `1 - p^K - (1-p)^K`. Compute this per problem from data t already logs (K=8) before spending compute on the next round, to see which of the 322 held-out and 189 pair problems are contributing gradient signal versus uniform pass/fail dead weight.

**Implementable estimator 2, difficulty band (STP, arXiv:2502.00212).** Draw K independent attempts per self-generated candidate (STP used K=32 for conjectures, K=64 for resampled existing statements), compute P̂, keep only P̂ ∈ (0, 1/4]. The plateau this breaks is quantified: at a matched checkpoint, expert iteration succeeded on 131 of 2.5M proof attempts on unproved statements (about 0.005%) while at least 47% of filtered self-play conjectures were proved; LeanWorkbook goes 13.2% (expert iteration, replicated by the authors) to 28.5%. Cost: 48 iterations, 51.3B tokens, 3.6M conjectures, 241M proofs, on DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5-SFT (7B). Two things do not port: STP's conjecturer only has to emit a statement that might be provable, with no obligation to produce a valid twin plus witness, and STP's seed pool is 89K statements against t's 785 lifted plus 449 in-fragment NL problems.

**Implementable procedure 3, model averaging (Goedel-Prover-V2, arXiv:2508.03613).** `θ_avg = (1−α)θ0 + αθ` where **θ0 is the original pretrained base, not the previous round's checkpoint**, applied after each SFT stage and after RL. The symptom it treats is stated directly: "in the later stages of SFT and RL, the model's diversity decreases. This is reflected by an increase in pass@1 but a decline in pass@N for larger values of N, such as N=32." The α sweep was {0.6, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9} over RL checkpoints {60, 80, 90}, and the paper is internally inconsistent about which side α weights, so verify direction empirically. **Two caveats before importing:** t has not yet logged this symptom (the one DPO round moved 16 to 18 at fixed N, and the inference path moved the numbers more), and the paper validates on full-parameter fine-tuning, not QLoRA, so blend on merged full-precision weights and re-check under t's inference path first. No numeric deltas are recoverable from the text; the evidence is the shape of Figure 8.

**The one honest calibration point for verified code at small scale is Tan.** 919 training examples, 16 prompts x 8 rollouts per batch, verl + GRPO/DAPO on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, held-out reward peaking at 24.7% at step 120 and falling to 9.7% by step 150 on the filtered set. **Do not adopt the train/held-out gap as a spec-hacking early warning:** it worked in the unfiltered run (+0.212 final versus +0.013 at best) and failed in the filtered run, where held-out sat above train (-0.050 at best, -0.007 at final) and the paper says outright "the degradation is not driven by a widening train-held-out gap". Also worth knowing before building a repair loop: in Tan's multi-turn runs 94.5-96.7% of verified trajectories succeeded on the first attempt, true post-failure repair occurred in 0.9-1.8% of trajectories, and 52.5% of failed-first trajectories collapsed to near-empty repairs.

**Reward shape if a repair loop is built:** `R_succ = γ^(T-1)`, γ=0.75, K=4 attempts, staged base 1.0 verify / 0.1 build-but-unverified / 0.0 build fail / -0.1 unparseable / -0.5 cheat detected, plus a linear overlength penalty (Tan). **GRPO coefficients as a comparison point:** KL 0.01, entropy 0.02, group size 4, temperature 1.0, batch 1024, lr 1e-5, with the warning that entropy regularisation raises pass@128 diversity but destabilises training after about 100 steps while KL-alone plateaus at SFT's best pass@16 (Re:Form).

**External anchors for how far there is to go:** VERINA v3, o3, 4.9% proof success at one trial per task; CLEVER, 1 of 161 (0.62%) end-to-end across GPT-4o, o4-mini, Claude-3.7, DeepSeek-R1, COPRA and a GPT-5-mini hybrid; FVAPPS, Sonnet 121/406 theorems (30%), Gemini 74/406 (18.5%), with one human spending 10 hours on sample 0023 and proving zero of its theorems.

### D. Translation fidelity

**Two mechanisms, both real, both conditional on a shared logic.** The Last Yard (Haselwarter, Hvass, Hansen, Winterhalter, Hriţcu, Spitters, CPP 2024, DOI 10.1145/3636501.3636961; IACR eprint 2023/185) contains **two different fidelity arguments**, and the distinction matters for how t frames its lifter:
- **Jasmin to SSProve** is a general theorem proved once in Coq against Jasmin's operational semantics, built on two general lemmas about stack-frame/heap relations plus call-site-parametric equivalence judgments. This closes a gap the earlier "Last Mile" work left open.
- **Hacspec to SSProve, cross-checked against Hacspec to functional Coq**, is **not** a once-proved theorem. It is translation validation by compositional symbolic evaluation: the tool walks the AST and generates a fresh Coq equivalence proof **per program**, kernel-checked. This is architecturally closer to what t's lifter already does (a certificate per task) than to "prove once and stop certifying". The honest framing for the paper is therefore "our per-task validation could be upgraded from differential testing to a machine-checked per-task equivalence proof", not "we could prove soundness once".
- **Both mechanisms require both translations to land in one logic (Coq).** t's seven kernels share no meta-logic in which "Dafny's proof of task X" and "Rocq's proof of task X" are even statable as equivalent. The technique would apply only to a currently-nonexistent scenario, two independent t lowerings into the same kernel, cross-validated inside it.
- Case study cost: an existing Jasmin AES-NI implementation proved conformant to a Hacspec NIST AES spec in about 2,500 lines of Coq, composed with SSProve's PRF-security theorem for IND-CPA.

**The counter-example is real and dated.** Holdsbjerg-Larsen, Milo, Spitters (CoqPL 2022, PDF at github.com/hacspec/hacspec.github.io/blob/master/coqpl22-final61.pdf, no arXiv ID) state: "we contribute an, as of yet, unverified printer which takes a formally verified ExprImp program and produces safe executable Rust." **Correction to the earlier framing of this citation:** the phrase "expected to be standard" belongs to a *different* gap in the same paper (hacspec's own operational-versus-denotational semantics), not to the printer, and SSProve is never mentioned in that paper at all. So do not cite it as "one hop proved, another unproved within one group's pipeline"; cite it for what it is, a self-reported admission that the final lowering to an executable target carries no fidelity check.

**Related but not on target.** hax (RustVerify 2024 extended abstract, 3 pages, no arXiv ID) is a toolchain description with zero quantitative results. Its one "proof" mention is a cross-backend refinement between two backend outputs (SSProve versus Coq) for one AES program, cited from the CPP paper, not a source-to-backend fidelity proof for any lowering target. Cite it at most for uneven cross-backend treatment across case studies. Aeneas (arXiv:2206.07185, ICFP 2022, DOI 10.1145/3547647) is the right kind of translator but its mechanisation status was not confirmed from the abstract in this search; **do not cite its mechanisation status without reading the body**. Crux (arXiv:2410.18280) is bit-precise symbolic equivalence against a Cryptol or hacspec spec, a third mechanism family, not translator validation.

### E. stdin to spec

**Directly portable acceptance gate: the round-trip rule, from Verus-SpecGym (arXiv:2605.26457, Sec. 3.1(A)).** For each task an agent (gpt5.3-codex in SWE-Agent) writes a parser R (raw testcase text to typed values) and a printer P, and "The parser is accepted only if every retained testcase round-trips: T_reproduced = P(R(t)) == t", byte-for-byte, with the agent revising R and P in a loop "until all retained testcases round-trip successfully, or the attempt is discarded". This is an ingestion-integrity gate, not a spec-fidelity check: it proves R and P agree on the retained cases, not that R implements the whole input grammar, and both could encode the same misreading. It is the right first gate for t's 20,509-problem stdin-shaped gap.

**Second portable gate: the four-bucket comp/sound split (same paper, Fig. 4).** Route each case into pre-completeness, pre-soundness, post-completeness, post-soundness, and score Pass@1 as accept-all-completeness AND reject-all-soundness, using symbolic verification first with a compiled-and-run fallback. Note this presupposes a hack/adversarial corpus, which t does not have. Model results for scale: Gemini 3.1 Pro 77.8%, other frontier 51.1-57.8%, open models 21.5-25.5%, on 581 tasks.

**Third portable gate: Post2Exe, from VeriContest (arXiv:2605.08553).** Compile Verus postconditions into executable code and run them against negative tests generated by semantic mutation, syntactic mutation (cargo-mutants) and output perturbation, targeting ten times as many negative as positive tests; this caught 60 incomplete postconditions for revision. Their suites average 252.7 positive tests per problem at 99.66% line coverage. Their headline capability drop, 92.18% plain code generation to 5.29% full verified, is the cost of adding spec and proof. Construction: 91 hand-built seeds expanded to 946 by a coding agent under three human checkpoints with a 20-minute agent budget.

**Fourth, cheapest gate: AxDafny's constant/empty-output vacuity mutation (arXiv:2606.32007).** No placeholder implementation and no auto-generated constant or empty-output mutant may verify, applied in 234 of 250 cases. Their LLM-judge semantic-mismatch rate against original problem statements, 14 of 250 (5.6%), is a citable external baseline for automated spec drafting fidelity. They also compile verified Dafny back to Python and run it against the original LiveCodeBench-Pro suite under original time limits.

**Pipeline shape, from FVAPPS (arXiv:2502.05714).** Aggregate reference tests, LLM drafts property tests with a capped retry loop against pytest, LLM converts tests to theorem statements with kernel typecheck retry, translate unit tests into kernel-native guard assertions and drop tasks whose reference solution fails them, then plausibility-filter with the kernel's own fuzzer. Tier counts 4,715 / 2,089 / 1,083. **One correction to carry:** the claim that FVAPPS deliberately restricts to call-based APPS and sidesteps stdin is **not in the paper**. Full-text grep for "call-based", "stdin", "fn_name" and "function signature" finds only an unrelated `stdin` hit describing the `lake build` subprocess. It may still be true of the dataset; verify against github.com/quinn-dougherty/fvapps or the HuggingFace dataset before citing it as a documented boundary condition.

**No standalone paper on input-format grammar parsing was found**; the technique exists only inside these benchmark-construction sections.

### F. Strings

**The one piece of real evidence bearing on seq-of-code-points.** Hu, Chen, Rümmer, Song, Wu, "Decision Procedure for A Theory of String Sequences" (arXiv:2509.00948) states that "existing SMT solvers support generic sequence theory but lack direct support for string-specific operations frequently used in string-manipulating code", and proves decidability for a straight-line fragment of a unified theory by encoding each string sequence as a string and each sequence operation as a string operation; implemented as ostrichseq on OSTRICH and validated against real JavaScript string constraints. Read plainly: a pure `seq<int>`-of-code-points encoding is fine for concatenation, slicing, indexing and functional update, and will need explicit modelling for regex matching, splitting and joining. Amadini's survey (arXiv:2002.02376) gives the taxonomy to position that choice against (word equations, automata-based theories, length arithmetic, sequence encodings).

**Everything else here is a gap, not a finding.** DafnyBench, VERINA and CLEVER papers report corpus size and success rates and, from the retrieved text, no per-datatype or per-operation breakdown. A string-density number for those corpora has to come from a local grep of the corpora and a walk of the ~161 Lean files in trishullab/clever and the VERINA task set, not from any paper. Verus (arXiv:2303.05491) is the correct citation for the `Seq<T>`-based spec-type architecture; the current state of Verus `String` verification support was **not** re-confirmed and should be checked against Verus source and docs rather than asserted.

### G. Tuples and effects in the IR

**Correction to the recipe, from a full read of Aeneas (arXiv:2206.07185).** The encoding is **one backward function per region (lifetime variable) in the function type, returning a tuple of every argument tied to that region**, not one per output-relevant argument. Their `choose(bool, &mut T, &mut T) -> &mut T` yields one `choose_fwd` and one `choose_back : ... -> result (t & t)` returning both updated arguments as a pair. Critically, **the effect-only case needs no backward function at all**: `fn ref_incr(x: &mut i32)` compiles to just `ref_incr_fwd : i32 -> result i32`, because the borrow terminates at the call site. The trigger for a backward function is an outstanding borrow that survives past the call, not effect-only-ness. Loops are **explicitly unsupported** in that paper ("We plan to address loops and disjunctions in the control-flow as soon as possible"), as are traits, closures and function pointers, and F* is the only implemented backend (Coq "in the works", Lean and HOL4 "planned"). Cost figures: hash table proved functionally correct in 201 LoC and 4 person-days in F*; Charon 9.5 kLoC Rust, Aeneas 13.5 kLoC OCaml.

**Aeneas's companion soundness paper does not cover the functional translation.** "Sound Borrow-Checking for Rust via Symbolic Semantics" (arXiv:2404.02680) proves PL refines LLBC, LLBC refines LLBC#, and that the new join operation preserves borrow-checking, adding about 3,000 lines of OCaml and testing on 22 micro-tests (300 LoC). It states in its own contributions list: "We leave a discussion of the correctness of the Aeneas functional translation to future work." Grep for "forward function" and "backward function" returns zero hits. **Do not cite it as backing the purify-effects-into-return-values encoding with a soundness result.** It proves the borrow-checking layer beneath that step.

**The counterweight, and its real strength.** Verus (SOSP '24, DOI 10.1145/3694715.3695952), Sec. 4.2.1: "the IronFleet code dealt with the painfulness of reasoning about fine-grained mutation by replacing an entire data structure with a modified version, leading to inefficient (and confusing) code. The Verus port simply uses an &mut reference, which expresses the original intent directly and avoids a performance penalty." This is a **one-paragraph qualitative developer report on one unnamed data structure**, with no isolating measurement, and the paper's own measured IronKV throughput (Figure 10) is captioned "The Verus version performs comparably to the IronFleet original". Verus's quantified wins (3-61x faster verification, 10x on IronKV, 95% smaller queries) are attributed to SMT-query engineering and EPR mode, not to mutation style. Quote it as a developer report, not as a benchmarked effect. **Where it does transfer to t:** if a kernel ever *executes* extracted code for the tests grading, a naive lowering of t's functional update inside a loop turns O(n) into O(n²), which is the same failure mode. That is our extrapolation, not the paper's claim, and it is checkable against LOOP-CURVE.md.

**Unverified background, flagged not cited.** Boogie's named out-parameters, WhyML's tuple returns plus region/effect tracking, and Viper's out-parameters over a permission-accounted heap were not re-verified this session. Primary sources to confirm before use: Barnett et al., Boogie (FMCO 2005); Filliâtre and Paskevich (ESOP 2013, already verified); Müller, Schwerhoff, Summers, Viper (VMCAI 2016).

## 3. Where our result is novel

Stated as searched-and-not-found, bounded by the search caveats at the end of this section.

1. **No published work found combining all three of: one source lowered to more than three independent kernels; a per-task mutant that every kernel must refute through a certificate that kernel accepts; and cross-kernel agreement as the headline metric.** The closest on (1) is AlgoVeri at 3 kernels with matched contracts, which has zero mutation/refutation content in its method and reports three side-by-side per-kernel columns. Bursuc et al. reach 3 kernels at larger scale with matched task IDs but never compute a cross-kernel consistency statistic. Why3 is many-backend but disjunctive and mutant-free. SV-COMP is many-tool over one C source with pre-labelled true/false variants rather than a per-task injected mutant. Falda reports convergence over ten axes on one proprietary system with the obligation-to-theorem correspondence explicitly not mechanised.

2. **No published work found that uses mutants or refuted twins as the negative training signal inside an RL or expert-iteration reward for verified code or spec generation.** The nearest analogues each stop short: Re:Form's subset reward checks implication against a single reference spec, Verus-SpecGym's adversarial cases are human-authored Codeforces hacks used at evaluation time only, Tan's anti-cheating check is a whitespace-normalised diff of contract text, and VeriContest's Post2Exe mutation is a benchmark-construction gate rather than a training signal. Searches for "AlgoVeri + mutant", "vericoding + mutant", "cross-prover", "multi-prover verification" and "verifier disagreement" returned nothing further; note that "multi-prover" is dominated by complexity-theory MIP results, not software verification.

3. **No published work found measuring string-operation coverage inside verified-code corpora.** No paper reports what fraction of DafnyBench, MBPP-DFY, HumanEval, VERINA or CLEVER tasks use concatenation versus slicing versus indexing versus regex. The DafnyBench, VERINA and CLEVER papers give size and success rates only.

4. **No paper found on string or character handling for Frama-C WP, SPARK/Ada, Rocq, or F* in this context**, and none comparing Dafny's UTF-16-code-unit `char` history against a full-code-point design. Dafny's `--unicode-char` discussion exists in project documentation, not in a citable paper.

5. **No standalone paper found on grammar-based or hybrid parsing of competitive-programming input formats as its own contribution**; the technique appears only inside benchmark-construction sections (Verus-SpecGym, VeriContest).

6. **No paper found applying CompCert-style translation validation to a Dafny/Verus/Lean/Rocq/F*/SPARK/Frama-C translation pair.** The Last Yard is the nearest, inside one Coq-hosted logic.

7. **No paper found reporting a per-round expert-iteration curve with stated data volume for verified code at 1-8B scale other than Tan (arXiv:2605.30914).** STP and DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5 give round-level mechanism and endpoints for Lean theorem proving at 7B; Goedel-Prover-V2 is 8B with headline numbers only; Kimina's 1.5B/7B distilled results were not broken out; Seed-Prover and DeepSeek-Prover-V2 are 72B-671B.

8. **No official third-party leaderboard found for any of the nine 2026 benchmarks searched.**

**Search-budget caveats that bound every statement above.** Several sub-searches ran with the session's WebSearch quota already exhausted (200/200) and against arXiv, Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex APIs returning 429s, so verification proceeded by direct fetches of arXiv abs/HTML/PDF, IACR eprint, Crossref, GitHub and Wayback captures rather than by broad search. Specific leads that could not be confirmed to exist or not, and are therefore omitted rather than guessed at: Beyer and Spiessl's later "Verification Witnesses" work; "Lean Kernel Arena" as a literature search term; "SAFE" (Verus) and "VeriBench" under those exact names (an arXiv search for "SAFE Verus proof repair" returned zero); a general-programming-language bucket on LLM code translation validated by differential testing.

## 4. Where our result conflicts with published work

1. **AlgoVeri (arXiv:2602.09464) holds priority on cross-kernel alignment.** Its Table 1 claims AlgoVeri "is the first to enforce strict parallel alignment across SMT-based (Dafny, Verus) and ITP-based (Lean) systems", marking VeriCoding and VerifyThisBench as unaligned. Any t sentence implying priority on aligned-contract multi-kernel comparison must yield to this. t's separable claims remain the twin/refutation requirement, the agreement-as-headline metric, and the kernel count.

2. **LeetProof / Velvet (arXiv:2604.16584) is a directly opposed design thesis with numbers behind it.** It names the same fragmentation problem and resolves it by unifying testing, SMT automation and interactive proof inside one Lean-embedded verifier, reporting 28/50 versus 17/50 fully proved against a single-mode Lean baseline at a fixed budget. t's cross-kernel answer has no comparable head-to-head against a unified-tool baseline. This is not a factual conflict but it is the competing answer a reviewer will raise, and it currently has an ablation that t does not.

3. **Tan (arXiv:2605.30914) contradicts the train/held-out gap as a spec-hacking detector.** The gap widened in the unfiltered run (+0.212 versus +0.013) and inverted in the filtered run (held-out above train, -0.050 and -0.007), where the paper states "the degradation is not driven by a widening train-held-out gap". Do not adopt this heuristic without validating it on t's own runs.

4. **Hu et al. (arXiv:2509.00948) conflicts with any claim that a code-point-sequence encoding is sufficient for strings.** Generic sequence theory "lacks direct support for string-specific operations frequently used in string-manipulating code" (regex matching, splitting, joining). t's `seq<int>` choice is fine for the operations t supports and will need explicit modelling if regex/split/join are ever wanted.

5. **Verus (SOSP '24) reports the value-only, whole-structure-replacement encoding as "inefficient (and confusing)" with a "performance penalty".** This is a counterweight to framing a single-return, value-only core as cost-free. It is qualitative and unmeasured, and the same paper's measured IronKV throughput is "comparable", so cite it as a developer report rather than a benchmark, but do not omit it.

6. **Aeneas (arXiv:2206.07185) conflicts with the recipe as previously summarised in our notes.** Backward functions are per **region**, tuple-valued, and the effect-only case with an immediately-terminating borrow needs **none**. Loops are unsupported in that paper. Any t prose describing "one backward function per output-relevant argument" must be corrected.

7. **arXiv:2404.02680 conflicts with using it as soundness backing for the purification encoding.** It explicitly defers "the correctness of the Aeneas functional translation" to future work.

8. **VeriScale (arXiv:2605.22368) conflicts with treating VERINA's headline numbers as a stable comparator.** Expanding VERINA's test suites 83x produces sharp score drops on both SpecGen and CodeGen across eight frontier models.

9. **FVAPPS (arXiv:2502.05714) does not say what our notes attributed to it.** The call-based-only, stdin-sidestepping boundary condition is not in the paper. If a manuscript sentence cites FVAPPS for that, it conflicts with the source and must be either removed or re-sourced from the dataset.

## 5. Reading list, prioritised

Read in this order. The reason given is why it must be read, not a substitute for reading it.

**Tier 1: read before the next draft of the cross-kernel claim.**

1. **AlgoVeri, arXiv:2602.09464.** Sec. 2.3 (alignment construction) and Table 1 (their explicit first-to-align claim), plus the "Necessity against degeneracy" sub-clause audit. This is the paper the novelty claim is positioned against, and their priority claim has to be read in their words before ours is written near it.
2. **Tan, arXiv:2605.30914.** Secs. 4.4 through 4.7, especially the "Experimental confounding" limitation and Tables 4.3, 4.6, 4.8, 4.10. Every number the manuscript quotes from this paper is qualified inside it, and one heuristic it appears to support is contradicted by its own second experiment.
3. **Re:Form, arXiv:2507.16331 (TMLR 05/2026).** Sec. 2.4 (subset reward definition), Table 10 (per-size effect), Fig. 9 (the reward-hacking curve), and the v4 failure-mode section. This is the concrete second spec-quality axis, and its precondition (a reference spec per task) determines which parts of t's task pool it can apply to.

**Tier 2: read before building the next gate or the next round.**

4. **Verus-SpecGym, arXiv:2605.26457.** Sec. 3.1(A) for the round-trip rule and Fig. 4 for the four-bucket split. The stdin front-end design for the 20,509-problem gap starts here, and App. F.7 carries the LLM-judge miss rate.
5. **Bursuc et al., arXiv:2509.22908.** Sec. 3.3 (SpecTranslator and the matched task IDs), Sec. 4 (the cheating validator), Table 2 (originals versus translations). Two reasons: the quality-score formula and cheating checklist are implementable, and our own citation of this paper needs correcting against Secs. 3.3 and 4.
6. **LeetProof, arXiv:2604.16584.** The introduction for the fragmentation framing (the competing thesis) and the PBT section for the type-directed output-mutation algorithm and the bounded-existential enumeration trick.
7. **VeriContest, arXiv:2605.08553.** The Phase III / Post2Exe section. Mutation-based postcondition completeness checking is the second orthogonal fidelity gate, and their negative-to-positive test ratio is a concrete target.

**Tier 3: read before the related-work section is fixed.**

8. **Hu, Chen, Rümmer, Song, Wu, arXiv:2509.00948.** The abstract and the fragment definition. This decides how the strings design is described and what the `seq<int>` encoding must claim.
9. **Aeneas, arXiv:2206.07185.** Sec. 4.3 (region abstractions), the `ref_incr` and `choose` examples, and Sec. 7 (Future Work, where loops are declared unsupported). Needed to correct the IR description.
10. **Beyer, SV-COMP 2024, DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-57256-2_15.** Table 1 (scoring schema) and the results-table column definitions. Needed to fix the over-claimed sentence with the actual mechanism.
11. **Haselwarter et al., The Last Yard, DOI 10.1145/3636501.3636961 / eprint 2023/185.** Sec. 5 (the Jasmin general theorem) versus the Hacspec translation-validation section. The distinction between prove-once and certify-per-program is exactly the distinction the lifter's framing turns on.
12. **CLEVER, arXiv:2505.13938.** The benchmark-construction section (annotation protocol, non-computable Prop design, Fig. 3 leaky-spec example, Fig. 8 FVAPPS counterexample). It is the strongest published statement that automatic spec extraction was judged too risky to use, with a cost figure attached.

**Tier 4: read before any round-2 training decision or SOTA table.**

13. **STP, arXiv:2502.00212** and **Goedel-Prover-V2, arXiv:2508.03613**, in that order. STP for the plateau mechanism and the (0, 1/4] difficulty band; Goedel-Prover-V2 Sec. 2.3 and Fig. 8 for diversity collapse and the averaging remedy, plus its caveats about full-parameter versus adapter training.
14. **Shefer et al., arXiv:2503.14183.** Short and directly useful: same HumanEval-derived problems, three kernels, one model, per-kernel spread reported (86% / 66-67% / 40%). The closest published template for how t should present its seven-column table.
15. **VeruSAGE, arXiv:2512.18436v2** and **AutoVerus, arXiv:2409.13082**, skimmed together. They bracket the task-distribution effect: over 90% on proof completion and over 80% on production proof debt against 24.7-44% on full generation.
